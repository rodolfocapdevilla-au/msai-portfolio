Mario Roldan

Investigador de física de partículas, experto en aceleradores de partículas y análisis de datos.

I am a theoretical particle physicist who builds physics-based numerical simulations and deep learning models for scientific data. In more than 11 years of research, including three years as a Research Associate at Fermilab, I have written more than 20 peer-reviewed papers. I am now Faculty Lead of the Masters Program in AI at Atlantis University, where I teach Deep Learning and Computer Vision.

Current Projects

Análisis de clima con anomaly detection

Muon Weather Observatory Network (MWON): a physics-informed autoencoder that detects anomalies in cosmic muon flux data from MicroBooNE, a Fermilab neutrino experiment, to identify and predict severe weather systems. I led the NASA NIAC grant application for MWON.

See the Fermilab poster on muon flux and storms

Estudio de materia oscura con neutrinos

Neutrino-Antineutrino Conversion from Ultralight Vector Dark Matter (arXiv:2605.06777, 2026), with A. Berlin, T. Cheng, M. Hostert and P. Machado. We show that Majorana neutrinos convert into antineutrinos in a background of ultralight vector dark matter coupled to lepton number, so a supernova neutrino burst seen at DUNE, Hyper-Kamiokande and JUNO could probe couplings beyond the reach of other future probes.

Read the paper on arXiv

Generador radiovoltaico

Radiovoltaic Generator: a nuclear-to-electricity power converter with a 1 kW output target. I developed a high-fidelity Monte Carlo radiation-transport simulation to model the device physics. Provisional Patent Application No. 63/874,784.

Selected Publications

Discovering Electroweak Interacting Dark Matter at Muon Colliders Using Soft Tracks

With F. Meloni and J. Zurita. We show that a 3 TeV muon collider can detect the soft tracks left by quasi-degenerate charged states, enabling the discovery of thermal Higgsinos and similar dark matter candidates. Phys. Rev. Lett. 134, 181802 (2025).

Read the paper on arXiv

Electroweak Symmetry Restoration and Radiation Amplitude Zeros

With T. Han. We propose using radiation amplitude zeros in Wγ, WZ and WH production at the LHC and muon colliders to measure how far electroweak symmetry is restored at high energy. Phys. Rev. Lett. 135, 101801 (2025).

Read the paper on arXiv

Building an AI-native Research Ecosystem for Experimental Particle Physics

A community whitepaper by 460 authors, including me, on how AI can accelerate discovery in experimental particle physics, from the HL-LHC and DUNE to a future muon collider in the U.S. arXiv:2602.17582 (2026).
