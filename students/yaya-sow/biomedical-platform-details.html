AI + Biomedical Data · Disease Prediction Platform

What This Project Is About

This project is an AI platform that predicts disease risk or progression by combining several different kinds of biomedical data about the same patients — instead of relying on just one. A person's health shows up differently in their genes, in their cells' activity, and in their clinical records, and each of those views is incomplete on its own. The goal here is to bring them together into a single model that sees the fuller picture.

In practice, that means starting from one clear medical question — for example, predicting diabetes risk or how far a cancer is likely to progress — and then gathering multiple types of real, public biomedical data that relate to it: genomic, transcriptomic, clinical, or environmental data, drawn from established repositories researchers already trust. Each of these datasets is cleaned and standardized on its own, since they arrive in very different shapes, and then merged into one unified dataset where every data type describes the same patients side by side.

Data Integration

Multiple biomedical data types — genomic, transcriptomic, clinical, and more — are cleaned and merged into one unified dataset per patient.

Prediction Model

An AI/ML model is trained on the integrated data to predict a disease outcome, and is checked for accuracy and interpretability.

User Interface

A simple dashboard lets researchers or clinicians upload data and see predictions, risk scores, and plain explanations.

That unified dataset is what trains the actual prediction model — an AI/ML system that learns to connect patterns across all those data types to a real outcome, such as a risk score or a progression stage. Because a model that works but can't explain itself is of limited use to a clinician, the project also checks why the model makes its predictions, not just how accurate they are, and ties its top signals back to biology that makes sense.

The end result isn't meant to stay a research script — it's built toward a simple, usable interface where a researcher or clinician can upload data and see predictions, risk scores, and visual explanations without needing to touch the underlying model themselves. Everything about the system — its data, its pipeline, its model choices, its limitations — is documented along the way, so the work can be reproduced, reviewed, or picked up by someone else.

In short: this project turns several separate, incomplete views of a patient's biology into one AI model that predicts disease outcomes — and makes that model usable by people who aren't the ones who built it.
